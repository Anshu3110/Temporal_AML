# Chapter 10: Q1 Journal Paper Writing Guide

This chapter provides a guide to converting your M.Tech dissertation into a high-impact manuscript suitable for submission to Q1 journals (e.g., IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, ACM Transactions on Privacy and Security, or Elsevier's Journal of Network and Computer Applications).

---

## 10.1 Structured Journal Sections

To meet Q1 review standards, structure your manuscript using these core sections:

### 10.1.1 Title and Abstract
*   **Title Guidelines**: The title should be specific and highlight your core technical contributions.
    *   *Draft*: *TemporalAML: Temporal Graph Attention Networks with Learnable Time Encodings for Explainable Cryptocurrency Anti-Money Laundering*
*   **Abstract Structure (200–250 words)**:
    1.  **Context**: Briefly state the rise of cryptocurrency and money laundering challenges.
    2.  **Problem Statement**: Explain the failure of traditional AML systems and static GNN models (e.g., temporal data leakage, loss of transaction velocity features).
    3.  **Proposed Methodology**: Introduce your model and highlight the learnable Fourier time encoding and multi-task prediction heads.
    4.  **Key Results**: State your main quantitative findings (e.g., *"...improves F1-score by 6.4% over static GNN models"*).
    5.  **Explainability**: Mention the compliance-ready explanations generated by GNNExplainer.

### 10.1.2 Introduction
*   Contextualize cryptocurrency ecosystems and the scale of illicit transaction volume.
*   Outline the research gaps in existing static GNN and heuristic-based AML systems.
*   List your core research contributions:
    1.  Constructing a continuous-time transaction graph from the Elliptic dataset.
    2.  Developing the learnable Fourier time encoding mechanism.
    3.  Implementing a multi-task learning head to detect three distinct laundering patterns.
    4.  Integrating GNNExplainer to generate compliance-ready subgraphs.

### 10.1.3 Literature Review
Organize your literature review into three themes:
1.  **Traditional Cryptocurrency Forensic Analysis**: Discuss heuristics, address clustering, and their limitations.
2.  **Static Graph Learning for AML**: Review static GCN and GraphSAGE models on transaction networks.
3.  **Temporal & Dynamic Graph Learning**: Discuss models like TGAT and TGN, and identify current research gaps (e.g., lack of learnable continuous-time encodings, lack of multi-pattern detection).

### 10.1.4 Methodology
Present your system architecture using rigorous mathematics. Include:
*   The continuous-time graph definition.
*   The learnable Fourier time encoding equations and frequency gradient derivations (detailed in Chapter 2).
*   The temporal neighborhood sampling algorithm.
*   The Multi-Task joint loss formulation.
*   The GNNExplainer optimization objective.

### 10.1.5 Experimental Setup
*   Detail the dataset splits (temporal split on step 34).
*   Describe your baseline models and hyperparameter settings (learning rate, hidden dimensions, batch sizes).
*   Define your evaluation metrics (Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and AUC-ROC) and explain how they handle class imbalance.

### 10.1.6 Results and Discussion
Present your results clearly using tables and charts:
*   **Table 1**: Overall performance comparison showing your model outperforming the baselines on F1 and AUC-ROC.
*   **Table 2**: Ablation studies evaluating the impact of the learnable time encoding (comparing learnable frequencies vs. fixed frequencies vs. no time encoding).
*   **Visualizations**: Show examples of GNNExplainer subgraphs, tracing the transaction flow of flagged nodes.
*   **Discussion**: Discuss how the learnable frequencies adapted to different transaction velocities during training.

### 10.1.7 Conclusion and Future Work
*   Summarize your key findings.
*   Acknowledge study limitations (e.g. relying on a single dataset).
*   Propose concrete directions for future research.

## 10.2 Q1 Journal Submission Guidelines

1.  **Write for Rigorous Reviewers**: Ensure every mathematical variable is defined, and provide detailed pseudocode for all algorithms.
2.  **Confirm Reproducibility**: Provide a link to your GitHub repository containing clean, documented code and instructions to download the dataset.
3.  **Respond Thoroughly to Reviewers**: Treat reviewer feedback as constructive guidance. Address every point in your response document and run additional experiments if requested.
