\section{Critical Review of Literature}
\label{sec:critical_review}

The academic trajectory of cryptocurrency anti-money laundering spans three distinct paradigms: static heuristic classification, discrete dynamic graph convolutions, and continuous-time geometric attention. This section evaluates these foundational streams, isolating the mathematical assumptions that impair their operational efficacy.

\subsection{Static Graph Neural Networks and Evaluation Leakage}

The primary direction of early graph-based AML research was established through spectral convolutions. Weber et al.~\cite{weber2019anti} demonstrated that localized topological convolutions on the Elliptic Bitcoin graph outperform flat tabular baselines (Random Forests, Logistic Regression), achieving illicit F1-scores near 0.70. In their formulation, however, all transaction events are combined into a single static adjacency matrix. By doing so, the network discards the chronological sequence and directionality of fund transfers. Applying standard non-temporal train/test splits introduces look-ahead evaluation leakage, where historical nodes aggregate messages from future blocks, generating artificially inflated metrics that fail under real-world streaming ledger deployments.

Later efforts attempted to model temporal drift through discrete snapshots at specific points in time. EvolveGCN~\cite{pareja2020evolvegcn} adapts the weights of GCNs over consecutive snapshot frames using Recurrent Neural Network (GRU/LSTM) updates. Although this design tracks parameter evolution at the macro level, partitioning continuous transaction streams into arbitrary snapshot windows discards intra-interval velocity. When a laundering chain executes multiple peeling transfers within a single hour, the structure cannot be resolved if the temporal resolution is summarized into two-week graph intervals.

\subsection{Graph Attention and Inductive Neighborhood Aggregation}

Graph Attention Networks (GAT)~\cite{velivckovic2018gat} introduced a technique that substituted static degree normalizations with parameterized self-attention mechanisms. By assigning dynamic attention weights to neighboring vertices, attention architectures model heterogeneous transaction distributions more effectively than isotropic convolutions. Hu et al.~\cite{hu2020gat} leveraged attention architectures to detect fraudulent structures in static financial topologies. Inductive frameworks such as GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} introduced localized neighborhood sampling, enabling inference across nodes where they have never been observed before, without retraining.

Despite these developments, traditional attention mechanisms handle all sampled neighbors as contemporaneous. An edge created minutes prior to an alert receives the identical temporal treatment as an edge confirmed weeks earlier. For financial surveillance, this uniform assumption discards elapsed time---one of the most discriminative signals that differentiates normal economic trade from fast, automated peeling chains.

\subsection{Continuous Dynamic Graph Networks and Harmonic Encoding}

To model continuous-time streaming topologies, Rossi et al.~\cite{rossi2020tgn} introduced the Temporal Graph Network (TGN) framework. TGN maintains dynamic internal memory states per vertex, updated sequentially with every incoming interaction. While theoretically expressive, maintaining and updating state vectors across hundreds of thousands of cryptocurrency addresses creates severe memory bottlenecks, limiting scalability during parallel batch training.

In parallel, Xu et al.~\cite{xu2020tgat} proposed the Temporal Graph Attention Network (TGAT). TGAT concatenates node attributes with continuous time-delta representations before computing multi-head attention weights. Drawing from Transformer sequence architectures~\cite{vaswani2017attention}, TGAT maps continuous elapsed time $\Delta t$ into a vector space using fixed harmonic sinusoids:
\begin{equation}
\omega_i = 10000^{-2i/d}, \quad i \in \{1, \ldots, d/2\}
\end{equation}
This fixed geometric progression assumes uniformly distributed temporal intervals typical of written language. Cryptocurrency ledgers diverge sharply from this assumption: transactions exhibit heavy-tailed, non-stationary burst dynamics with extreme velocity clustering. Fixed geometric frequencies cannot adjust to these domain-specific temporal distributions.

\subsection{Architectural Decoupling in GNN-LSTM Hybrids}

Hybrid frameworks frequently combine spatial graph operations with recurrent sequence processing. Kim et al.~\cite{kim2022gnn} evaluated GCN-LSTM combinations on synthetic AML datasets, confirming that tracking both topology and sequence improves fraud detection. Their design, however, decouples structural and temporal modeling into sequential modules: spatial embeddings are computed first, and time-series layers process historical sequences downstream. Consequently, edge-level time deltas never modulate the spatial aggregation step. In contrast, embedding temporal vectors directly into the attention coefficient computation allows elapsed time to directly govern how much weight is placed on each neighbor.

\subsection{Theoretical Foundations of Learnable Harmonic Projections}

The mathematical basis for mapping continuous intervals into vector spaces rests on Bochner's Theorem~\cite{bochner1933monotone}. The theorem states that any continuous, stationary shift-invariant kernel $K(t_1, t_2) = \psi(t_1 - t_2)$ can be expressed as the Fourier transform of a finite positive Radon measure $\Lambda$:
\begin{equation}
K(t_1, t_2) = \int_{\mathbb{R}} e^{i \omega (t_1 - t_2)} \, d\Lambda(\omega)
\end{equation}
While prior temporal models approximate this integral using fixed Monte Carlo samples or rigid geometric sequences, TemporalAML treats the Fourier frequency spectrum $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and phase shifts $\boldsymbol{\phi}$ as learnable parameters. By optimizing these harmonic bases via backpropagation, the model discovers frequency bands corresponding to specific laundering velocities.

\subsection{Forensic Subgraph Explainability}

Typical GNN structures are opaque predictive systems that produce significant friction for compliance processes requiring auditable documentation. Ying et al.~\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer} formulated GNNExplainer, an optimization method that isolates compact, faithful subgraphs by maximizing mutual information between full-graph predictions and masked sub-topologies. Subsequent studies~\cite{giese2022explainable, lo2023gnn} applied post-hoc attribution methods to financial risk scoring.

Current explainability methods for cryptocurrency AML have two main limitations: they only explain binary illicit scores, and they do not consider the chronological sequence of the extracted subgraphs. By integrating GNNExplainer directly with multi-typology prediction heads, TemporalAML isolates time-ordered evidence subgraphs tailored to specific laundering processes, facilitating the direct synthesis of Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs).

\section{Summary of Literature}
\label{sec:lit_summary}

Across the reviewed corpus, methodological development exhibits distinct inflection points. Initial works demonstrated the utility of graph representations for illicit entity detection (Weber et al.~\cite{weber2019anti}), but relied on static projections that discard transaction sequencing. Subsequent architectures introduced temporal dynamics through discrete snapshot recurrence (EvolveGCN~\cite{pareja2020evolvegcn}) or continuous harmonic attention (TGAT~\cite{xu2020tgat}, TGN~\cite{rossi2020tgn}). None of these frameworks, however, optimize frequency parameters directly against cryptocurrency transaction streams. Concurrently, explainability methods (GNNExplainer~\cite{ying2019gnnexplainer}, Giese et al.~\cite{giese2022explainable}) establish the feasibility of isolating compact subgraphs, yet remain confined to generic binary classifications without temporal ordering.

Prior research leaves a notable intersection unaddressed: no single framework unifies learnable continuous Fourier attention, simultaneous multi-typology AML classification, and time-ordered forensic subgraph extraction under a leak-free protocol. TemporalAML targets this exact gap.


\section{Identification of Gaps}
\label{sec:gaps}

A rigorous review of the domain isolates five structural deficiencies:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Loss of Temporal Sequence in Static Graphs:} Conventional GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE pipelines discard edge timestamps, obscuring transfer velocity and generating evaluation leakage when evaluated across arbitrary train/test partitions.
	\item \textbf{Rigid Harmonic Parameterization:} Existing continuous models fix Fourier bases using geometric NLP constants that cannot adapt to the bursty, power-law inter-arrival dynamics of blockchain transactions.
	\item \textbf{Coarse Binary Prediction:} Literature benchmarks almost exclusively predict undifferentiated illicit/licit labels, failing to differentiate operational typologies like layering chains, cyclic flows, or fan-out structures.
	\item \textbf{Absence of Chronological Subgraph Evidence:} Current graph explainers provide uncalibrated node importance masks without time-ordered sequencing, preventing immediate integration into regulatory filing formats.
	\item \textbf{Look-Ahead Information Leakage:} Pervasive reliance on randomized cross-validation permits future edges to influence past node classifications, overestimating operational detection rates.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Need for Research}
\label{sec:need_for_research}

Addressing these challenges necessitates an integrated framework capable of: (i) modeling continuous transaction intervals directly within graph attention; (ii) optimizing temporal frequency parameters via backpropagation to discover laundering velocity profiles; (iii) classifying distinct structural typologies simultaneously from shared latent representations; and (iv) extracting chronologically indexed subgraph evidence suitable for compliance filings. TemporalAML resolves each requirement within a unified, leak-free design.


\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:lit_problem_statement}

Existing Anti-Money Laundering architectures in cryptocurrency analytics fail to detect multiple structural typologies—Circular Transfers, Layering Chains, and Smurfing/Fan-Out behaviors—concurrently over continuous temporal graphs. In addition, existing frameworks cannot output time-ordered, explainable subgraph evidence required for regulatory SAR submissions without incurring evaluation leakage. This research develops TemporalAML, a Temporal Graph Attention Network with trainable Fourier time encoding, to resolve these operational shortcomings.


\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:lit_objectives}

\begin{enumerate}
	\item[\textbf{O1}] Construct a directed, weighted temporal graph $G = (V, E, \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{T})$ from the Elliptic Bitcoin benchmark containing 203,769 vertices, 234,355 directed edges, 172 attributes per node (incorporating six custom topological features), and 49 sequential block intervals, partitioned under a strictly chronological, leak-free protocol.
	
	\item[\textbf{O2}] Formulate and implement a Learnable Fourier Time Encoding module featuring trainable frequencies $\boldsymbol{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^{d/2}$ and phase shifts $\boldsymbol{\phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{d/2}$ embedded inside the attention mechanism, allowing backpropagation to calibrate the network to characteristic transaction velocities.
	
	\item[\textbf{O3}] Develop a two-layer Temporal Graph Attention Network (TGAT) with a shared 128-dimensional latent temporal manifold and three specialized heads detecting Circular Transfers, Layering Chains, and Smurfing behaviors, trained under a joint class-weighted multi-task objective.
	
	\item[\textbf{O4}] Integrate GNNExplainer to optimize edge attribution masks $M \in [0,1]^{|E_s|}$ by maximizing mutual information, deriving time-ordered, typology-specific subgraph evidence for automated SAR reporting.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent \textit{Note: Objective 5—Comprehensive baseline benchmarking against GCN, GraphSAGE, GNN-LSTM, and static GAT models alongside full metric ablations—constitutes the research program for Dissertation Phase 2.}


\section{Limitations of the Study}
\label{sec:limitations}

As an empirical investigation conducted in Dissertation Phase~1, the following methodological boundaries apply:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Benchmark Boundary:} Experimental validation relies exclusively on the Elliptic Bitcoin Dataset. Evaluating cross-chain transferability to account-based ledgers (e.g., Ethereum) or privacy-preserving networks (e.g., Monero) remains future work.

	\item \textbf{Transaction vs.\ Entity Resolution:} The Elliptic benchmark maps individual transactions as nodes rather than entity wallets. Consequently, circular laundering patterns—which describe value cycles across user wallets—manifest as DAG structures without directed cycles at the transaction level. This topological property is an inherent constraint of UTXO transaction graphs rather than an algorithmic flaw.

	\item \textbf{Heuristic Typology Annotation:} Ground-truth supervisory signals for Layering and Smurfing patterns are extracted through topological graph mining (directed BFS paths and degree cutoffs) applied to known illicit clusters, rather than manual forensic annotations. This introduces label noise that must be considered during evaluation.

	\item \textbf{Benchmarking Roadmap:} Comparative benchmarking against GCN, GraphSAGE, GNN-LSTM, and static baselines, along with full F1, AUC-ROC, and temporal drift evaluations, is scheduled for Phase~2.

	\item \textbf{Explanation Metric Audits:} Quantitative assessments of GNNExplainer outputs (measuring explanation fidelity, edge sparsity, and regulatory compliance utility) are deferred to Phase~2.
\end{enumerate}
